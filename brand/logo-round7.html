Bodoni. Black. Proportion.

Giambattista Bodoni cut his types in Parma in 1790 and they are still what Italian luxury sets its name in. Everything here is built from his letterforms: thick verticals, hairline serifs, a ruthless vertical axis. No mint, no icons, no tricks — the elegance is in the spacing. One idea, three executions.

I. The ligature

A Bodoni K and R drawn into one another: the R steps forward, its stem landing exactly where the K's arms meet it, a hairline of white between the two so the eye can separate them. Two letters, one weight, one baseline. This is the mark.

II. The seal

The same ligature set inside a fine double ring, the name running around it in spaced capitals — the way a Florentine or Milanese house marks its paper, its keys and its doors. This is the version for the favicon, the app icon, an embossed folder, a brass plate.

III. The initial

Hierarchy instead of interlock: a large K, and the R set small and precise in the angle of its arms — the way a monogram is stitched on linen. Quieter than the ligature, and the most "old house" of the three.
